What changed, and why

AreaRefinementReason / tradeoff
Control placementView → Selection → Events stays in one compact group. On wide windows it sits beside the period; in a smaller window the entire group moves below it. Navigation stays immediately left of the date.The groups keep their order and proximity rather than occupying separate ends of the screen.
Header alignmentThe period title and group labels share a text baseline. The range and navigation align with the control row beneath. The period title is 22px rather than 24px.Two clear alignment rows give the title less visual weight while preserving hierarchy. The date column reserves enough width for every month.
Color rolesNeutral weekend labels and backgrounds replace the bright red mini-calendar dates and inherited green surface fragments. Source colors remain on events and source bars; metadata uses one slate color.A muted terracotta current-time marker is an intentional semantic accent. It now sits behind cards, so it cannot cross out an event title.
List alignmentTop-level calendar and agenda/search source bars share a column; names and event titles share another. Nested search occurrences keep their indentation and folding controls.Alignment communicates their relationship to a calendar. One-line rows with eye controls versus two-line event rows already distinguish their roles.
Selection & surfacesBoth lists use the same quiet green row fill, subtle outline, and rounded corners. Calendar visibility stays a separate, unfilled eye control. Focus has its own dotted outline.B1 relies on whitespace. B2 adds one light panel around each list, without boxing every row. Main-calendar selection retains the source hue.
Button bordersOrdinary icon actions have no resting box. Fields are outlined; exclusive choices sit inside a segmented container, with a white chosen segment. Hover adds a quiet tint.Consistency means the same treatment for the same role, rather than a border around every clickable object.
Spacing & cornersA 4px spacing scale: 8px from headings to content, 16px between command groups, 24px between major sidebar sections, and 20px outer margins. Small controls and row selections use 4px corners; groups and panels use 6px.Whitespace provides enough section separation in B1. B2 tests subtle outlines as an alternative; adding divider lines as well would be redundant.
All-day clutterThe repeated “+ All day” labels disappear. A single ALL DAY axis label and the pinned lane remain, along with connected multi-day bars.Existing creation gestures remain available. A permanent action label in every day adds little once the lane is visible.
Grid linesOne neutral line color and continuous column boundaries through weekday headers, the all-day lane, and the hours. Pinned surfaces reserve the timed scrollbar’s width.Continuous geometry is easier to follow. The lines remain light so the calendar does not compete with its events.
TypographyWeekday labels use the same 9pt semibold style in week, month, and import. Week date numbers remain larger than month-cell dates. Body text, metadata, and section labels have consistent roles.The weekday is the same information in each view; the date number has a different hierarchy in a seven-column header versus a 42-cell month.

What to compare

  • Wide versus smaller window: do the header groups feel stable and related?
  • Open lists versus panels: is whitespace enough to make the lists clear?
  • Search: do selected rows read consistently with the creation calendar?
  • Week versus month: is the shared typography and line work calmer?
  • Yellow calendar: titles and times should stay readable.
Scope, sources, and reproduction

These are static native Qt renderings, using an in-memory database, disabled sync, and synthetic events frozen to 6 November 2026. No production files, profiles, or calendar data were changed. This round refines B; it does not switch to C’s near-white event cards.

The prototypes check visual fit at two sizes. Long event-title clipping in narrow columns remains visible rather than being hidden. They do not establish complete accessibility or interaction conformance.

  • Proximity and similarity inform grouping and repeated control roles.
  • GNOME typography guidance informs the smaller set of type roles.
  • W3C text contrast and non-text contrast inform readability and state indicators; decorative gridlines can stay quiet.
