Answers and tradeoffs ↓

What I would defend strongly

  • Readable event text while preserving the server’s source colors.
  • Predictable group placement, with navigation beside the date.
  • Clear current-source, selected-event, visibility, and keyboard-focus states.
  • Accurate column geometry, including scrollbar space.

These constrain usability and the app’s intended behavior. They do not dictate a particular tint, radius, or container style.

What remains a design choice

  • Calendars and events can share a grammar or intentionally look different.
  • Weekends can use warm/cool shading or a quieter red text cue.
  • Whitespace, dividers, and surfaces are different ways to group content.
  • A header line break or modest type-size difference can have a purpose.

Consistency is a coherent relationship between appearance and meaning; it does not require making different things look identical.

The choices being tested

Bundling choices makes the full app visible, but does not prove which individual choice caused a preference. The component views above let us compare the questions separately.

My provisional reading

I lean toward distinguishing the creation-calendar controls from event objects, because they change different kinds of state. That does not require adopting the card density of Sources & agenda: its time column and tinted cards are an experiment, not a prerequisite.

I favor a restrained weekend cue and an integrated all-day creation affordance. I do not see a universal winner between warm shading, cool shading, and muted red labels, or between a continuous grid and an open date header. The flatter direction is intentionally useful as a counterpoint: less framing can also mean weaker discoverability.
