Applied to the app

Sidebar and calendar

  • Larger source-color squares; the creation calendar is bold and underlined, without a selection box.
  • Source-tinted event cards, with readable text, distinct selection/focus, and no timeline.
  • Clear overview / calendars / agenda-search regions, using B’s pane and separators.
  • Muted weekend text with no shaded weekend columns.
  • Continuous week/day boundaries, including pinned all-day and timed lanes with either scrollbar.
  • No repeated + All day row or hover plus. Existing creation gestures remain.

Preferences and scope

  • Preferences → Appearance → Color scheme: Neutral, Warm, or Cool.
  • The choice persists per profile and applies to calendar views, overview, editor/import previews, and date pickers.
  • Server calendar colors remain source data and are preserved.
  • Individual icon actions are borderless; selected segments no longer have a second inner outline.
  • Header placement retains the existing app baseline pending the choices below.
  • Search stays above the results heading until a placement is chosen.

Full suite: 1,328 tests and 135 subtests passed, including the launch/import tests requiring local IPC sockets.

Header choices · H1–H7

The first six cross three navigation layouts with two selected-state treatments. All keep the command groups together and use A/B-style vertical alignment. H7 separately tests tighter spacing while retaining those alignment rows.

How I would compare these: H3/H4 answer the month-alignment and selected-style questions directly. H1/H2 preserve the familiar navigation cluster. H5/H6 keep that cluster together while aligning only the month; the range is indented. H7 tests whether a smaller title and tighter controls improve the vertical balance. Today’s placement is deliberately visible in every option; none is silently chosen for the app.

Search placement · S1–S4

Compare both the resting agenda and an active query. These options preserve Ctrl+F, source visibility, filtering, series folding, and the main calendar’s width.

S1 is the applied baseline. S2 saves a row with shorter Agenda/Results captions. S3 gives the resting agenda a cleaner heading but requires a click or Ctrl+F to expose search. S4 makes search look global, at the cost of distancing the field from its results. My preference is to compare S2’s persistent field with S3’s quieter resting state.

Day structure, with or without a timeline · A1–A3

The app currently uses A1. These additional experiments test the day-break issue before deciding whether any timeline belongs in Next events. A2/A3 deliberately revert to ordinary event cards during search.

Day headings can make dates easier to scan without a timeline. A3 demonstrates short rails that stop at day boundaries, rather than joining unrelated days. Both add structure and consume space; A1 remains a reasonable choice if consistency and compactness matter more.
